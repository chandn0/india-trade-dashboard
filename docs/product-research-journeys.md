# Product Research Journeys

These are structured dry runs of the current Product Composition and
Buildability workspaces against five decisions from the Product Decision
Charter. They are not user-research interviews. Their purpose is to identify
where the present data supports a conclusion and where the next evidence pilots
must intervene.

Values are FY2025-26 US$ unless stated otherwise.

## Journey 1 — Should integrated circuits be treated as buildable?

**Product:** HS 8542  
**Question type:** Manufactured intermediate input  
**Current evidence level:** E2 — reviewed attribution and trade evidence

### What the dashboard answers

| Measure                            |         Current result |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------: |
| Imports                            |               $30.32bn |
| Exports                            |                $0.28bn |
| Net deficit                        |              −$30.04bn |
| FY2021-22 imports                  |               $13.64bn |
| Five-year import increase          |              +$16.69bn |
| Production stage                   |     Intermediate input |
| Buildability                       |       Partly buildable |
| Primary lever                      | Domestic manufacturing |
| Horizon                            |             7–10 years |
| Current realistic net-impact range |        $3.00bn–$7.51bn |

The dashboard correctly prevents the $30.32bn gross import value from becoming
a $30.32bn “opportunity.” It identifies a large, widening, low-offset component
gap and applies a partial, long-horizon interpretation.

### What remains unanswered

- Which chip categories make up the HS-4 total?
- Which partner countries dominate the product-specific supply?
- How much packaging, testing, mature-node fabrication, and design capacity
  already exists domestically?
- What imported equipment, wafers, gases, and materials would new domestic
  output require?
- How much of the component import supports exported phones and electronics?
- Are the current scenario percentages evidence-derived or judgmental?

### Decision today

**Research-ready, not decision-ready.** Integrated circuits deserve deeper
investigation, but the correct opportunity is not “make all chips.” It must be
narrowed to tractable component and process segments.

### Required next evidence

This journey directly justifies the Electronics Evidence Pilot (S06): partner ×
HS-4, deeper product detail, domestic capacity, imported-input requirements, and
policy scope.

## Journey 2 — Is edible-oil dependence caused by price or volume?

**Products:** HS 1511, 1507, and 1512  
**Question type:** Agricultural commodity  
**Current evidence level:** E2 for value and classification; E0 for quantity

### What the dashboard answers

| Product                    |      Imports |     Exports |   Net deficit |
| -------------------------- | -----------: | ----------: | ------------: |
| Palm oil                   |      $9.59bn |     $0.05bn |      −$9.54bn |
| Soybean oil                |      $6.21bn |     $0.04bn |      −$6.16bn |
| Sunflower and related oils |      $3.32bn |     $0.12bn |      −$3.21bn |
| **Combined**               | **$19.12bn** | **$0.21bn** | **−$18.91bn** |

The dashboard identifies these products as agricultural commodities, growable
only in part, with crop-yield expansion as the main lever and five-to-ten-year
horizons.

Value histories are not uniform:

- Palm imports fell from $10.17bn in FY2021-22 to $9.59bn.
- Soybean-oil imports rose from $5.53bn to $6.21bn.
- Sunflower and related oils rose from $2.94bn to $3.32bn.

### What remains unanswered

The current value data cannot determine whether these changes came from:

- physical import volume
- international oil prices
- product mix
- exchange-rate effects
- domestic harvest and crushing conditions

It also cannot calculate apparent consumption or defensible import dependence
without domestic production and compatible physical units.

### Decision today

**Large and net-relevant, but the mechanism is unresolved.** The page can rank
the deficit but cannot yet tell whether current dependence is improving.

### Required next evidence

This journey justifies the Edible-Oils Evidence Pilot (S07): quantities, unit
values, partner concentration, domestic oilseed/oil production, yield, and
apparent consumption.

## Journey 3 — Does telecom export growth improve the net position?

**Product:** HS 8517  
**Question type:** Finished electronics / mixed telecom heading  
**Current evidence level:** E2 for trade; classification needs a mixed-use note

### What the dashboard answers

| Measure     | FY2021-22 | FY2025-26 |                Change |
| ----------- | --------: | --------: | --------------------: |
| Imports     |  $14.00bn |  $21.72bn |              +$7.72bn |
| Exports     |   $7.11bn |  $33.76bn |             +$26.65bn |
| Net balance |  −$6.89bn | +$12.04bn | +$18.93bn improvement |

At HS-4 level, export growth has more than offset the simultaneous increase in
imports. The line moved from deficit to a large surplus.

### What the dashboard correctly prevents

It prevents a reader from describing the $21.72bn import line as a standalone
import-substitution opportunity. The export counter-flow is larger than the
current import value.

### What remains unanswered

- HS 8517 contains phones, network equipment, and related apparatus; it is not a
  pure smartphone series.
- The domestic value-added share of the export is unknown.
- Imported components supporting exported devices sit in other HS-4 lines.
- Product-specific partner sourcing is unavailable.
- The page cannot allocate the electronics value-chain net position across
  phones, semiconductors, displays, batteries, and other parts.

### Decision today

**The net result improved materially, but domestic value addition is unresolved.**
The correct next question is how much of the export surplus remains after
imported components—not whether imports should simply be cut.

### Required next evidence

This belongs inside S06 and should be tested through an electronics value-chain
account rather than a single-product substitution scenario.

## Journey 4 — Which scrap imports are realistic recycling opportunities?

**Products:** HS 7204, 7602, and 7404  
**Question type:** Recovered raw materials  
**Current evidence level:** E2 — reviewed attribution and Buildability evidence

### What the dashboard answers

| Product         |      Imports |     Exports |   Net deficit | Buildability |
| --------------- | -----------: | ----------: | ------------: | ------------ |
| Ferrous scrap   |      $4.56bn |     $0.02bn |      −$4.54bn | Recoverable  |
| Aluminium scrap |      $4.55bn |     $0.05bn |      −$4.50bn | Recoverable  |
| Copper scrap    |      $3.43bn |     $0.32bn |      −$3.11bn | Recoverable  |
| **Combined**    | **$12.54bn** | **$0.39bn** | **−$12.15bn** |              |

All three products have:

- a clear material-recovery mechanism
- low export offsets
- one-to-three-year horizons
- high classification confidence
- combined directional net-impact ranges of roughly $2.43bn–$4.86bn

### What remains unanswered

- Domestic scrap generation by metal
- Collection and formalisation rates
- Quality/grade requirements of Indian users
- Recoverable versus already-recovered domestic material
- Cost of sorting, contamination control, and logistics
- Whether reducing scrap imports would displace lower-carbon secondary metal
  production with primary metal

### Decision today

**Strongest current cross-product research shortlist.** Scrap is more tractable
than many larger lines, but the opportunity is a collection and quality system,
not a simple import restriction.

### Required next evidence

A later recycling pilot should join domestic waste-flow and secondary-metal
capacity data. It ranks behind the two selected pilots because those pilots test
more of the general evidence architecture.

## Journey 5 — Which large imports should not be direct substitution targets?

**Question type:** Exclusion discipline  
**Current evidence level:** E2

### Case A — Crude petroleum, HS 2709

- Imports: $134.72bn
- Exports: negligible
- Net deficit: −$134.72bn
- Classification: energy input
- Buildability: structurally imported
- Lever: demand shaping
- Horizon: 10+ years

**Conclusion:** It is the largest deficit line but not a conventional domestic
manufacturing opportunity. Electrification, efficiency, and demand substitution
are the relevant mechanisms.

### Case B — Diamonds, HS 7102

- Imports: $16.41bn
- Exports: $12.44bn
- Net deficit: −$3.96bn
- Classification: raw material
- Buildability: structurally imported
- Lever: export upgrading

**Conclusion:** Gross imports are productive inputs into a major export value
chain. Import compression could damage exports; net value addition is the
correct lens.

### Case C — Aircraft, HS 8802

- Imports: $12.83bn
- Exports: $0.07bn in the latest year
- Net deficit: −$12.76bn
- Classification: capital good
- Buildability: partly buildable
- Horizon: 10+ years

**Conclusion:** The line is large, but aircraft are productive transport assets
and globally concentrated systems. Local content and capability accumulation
are more realistic than near-term substitution.

### Case D — Copper concentrates, HS 2603

- Imports: $5.94bn
- Exports: $0.04bn
- Net deficit: −$5.90bn
- Classification: raw material
- Buildability: structurally imported
- Lever: sourcing diversification

**Conclusion:** Domestic smelting can add value without eliminating imported ore
dependence. Resource endowment constrains the substitution claim.

### Decision today

The dashboard successfully distinguishes **deficit size** from **opportunity
quality** in all four cases. This exclusion function is as important as finding
buildable products.

# Cross-journey findings

## What already works

- Gross and net values remain visible together.
- Stage attribution changes interpretation.
- The Buildability taxonomy prevents universal substitution claims.
- Scenario ranges are applied to net deficits rather than gross imports.
- Evidence availability is visible.

## What creates the most decision uncertainty

1. Partner concentration at true HS-4 level
2. Price versus physical volume
3. Domestic production and capacity
4. Imported-input content of exports
5. Mixed-use HS-4 headings

## UI that can wait

No new general-purpose chart is necessary before the two evidence pilots. The
current product explorer, profiles, comparison tool, signals, and scenario
laboratory are sufficient to test the decision workflow.

## Recommended implication

Proceed with the Electronics and Edible-Oils evidence pilots only after the
validator and classification-confidence work are complete. Use the pilots to
decide whether broader data acquisition deserves maintenance investment.
