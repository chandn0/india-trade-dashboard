# India Merchandise Trade — Patterns & Long-Term Trends

_Analysis of India's goods imports and exports, FY 2009‑10 to FY 2025‑26 (17 fiscal years)._
_Prepared June 2026. All values in US$ million / billion unless stated. Fiscal year = April–March._

---

## Scope, sources & method

- **Coverage:** Merchandise (goods) trade only — sourced from DGCIS / TRADESTAT (FTPA commodity‑group reports, FTSPCC country‑wise totals, and the TIA HS‑4 extraction). **Services trade is excluded** (see caveat at the end — it materially changes the deficit story).
- **Headline annual totals** in this note use the FTPA **commodity‑group annual totals** because they provide one continuous FY 2009‑10 to FY 2025‑26 series. FTSPCC March‑cumulative totals and HS‑4 sums are used as cross‑checks where coverage overlaps, but they are not perfectly identical in every year.
- **Data note:** the convenience file `india_trade_yearly_summary.csv` stores FTSPCC **full fiscal‑year March‑cumulative totals** for FY 2010‑11 onward. This document does **not** rely on that CSV as its sole annual series; it uses the commodity‑group annual totals for the 17‑year headline table and HS‑4 totals for the FY22–FY26 item analysis. See [Data notes](#data-notes--caveats).

---

## Executive summary

1. **Trade roughly tripled in 16 years, but imports outran exports.** Exports grew $178.8 bn → $441.7 bn (2.5×, 5.8% CAGR); imports grew $288.4 bn → $775.0 bn (2.7×, 6.4% CAGR). India ran a merchandise deficit **every single year**.
2. **The deficit has widened structurally** — from $109.6 bn (FY10) to a record **$333.2 bn (FY26)**, a 3.0× increase. Cumulative merchandise deficit over the period ≈ **$3.0 trillion**.
3. **Export coverage is at a record low.** Exports covered 57.0% of imports in FY26 — the weakest in the 17‑year series (vs a ~72% high in FY17).
4. **The export basket transformed from resources/labour toward engineering & electronics.** Electronics (3.1%→10.9%), Machinery (5.6%→11.1%) and Chemicals (9.6%→14.0%) gained heavily; **Gems & Jewellery (16.1%→6.4%) and Textiles (13.0%→8.1%) lost the most.**
5. **Imports are still oil‑led but electronics is the rising force.** Petroleum's share fell from 30.2% to 22.4% (still #1), while Electronics surged 7.9%→15.0% to become #2.
6. **The smartphone is the single biggest recent export story:** HS‑4 line 8517 went $7.1 bn → $33.8 bn in five years (+375%). Meanwhile **gold (+56%) and semiconductors (+122%) drive the import bill higher.**
7. **A 3‑year export plateau (FY24–FY26, ~$437–442 bn) against still‑rising imports** is turning the deficit from an oil‑price _cyclical_ into a _structural_ gap.
8. **The rupee nearly halved** against the dollar (₹45.6 → ₹88.3 per USD avg), so dollar‑terms growth understates rupee‑terms growth — and inflates the USD‑priced import bill (oil, gold, chips).

**What the path shows:**

- **Two distinct regimes.** A volatile $250–330 bn export band from FY11–FY20, then a step‑change after COVID to a $420–450 bn band from FY22 onward. Imports show the same break, stepping from a ~$450–514 bn band to $613–775 bn.
- **Imports compound faster than exports** (6.4% vs 5.8% CAGR). Small as the gap looks, over 16 years it is exactly why the deficit tripled while trade only ~2.5×'d.

## 2. Anatomy of the deficit

**Persistent and widening.** India has not posted a single merchandise surplus year in this window. The deficit's trajectory is the clearest long‑term signal in the data:

- **Export coverage ratio (exports ÷ imports) is deteriorating.** It oscillated 61–72% for most of the 2010s, spiked to 74% in the COVID year (imports collapsed faster), then fell steadily to a **record‑low 57.0% in FY26.** Exports now finance a _smaller_ share of the import bill than at any point since FY10.
- **Cumulative deficit ≈ $3.0 trillion** over FY10–FY26.

**Deficit is cyclically sensitive to two things — oil prices and import demand:**

| Episode                              | What happened                                              | Deficit effect                                                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **FY15–FY16 oil crash**              | Crude prices collapsed; both flows fell ~15% in FY16       | Deficit _narrowed_ to ~$119 bn                                     |
| **FY21 COVID shock**                 | Imports −16.9% (vs exports −6.9%) as domestic demand froze | Deficit _narrowed_ sharply to $102.6 bn — the lowest of the period |
| **FY22 reopening + commodity surge** | Exports +44.6%, imports +55.4%                             | Deficit jumped to $191 bn                                          |
| **FY23 commodity/energy spike**      | Record oil & gold bills                                    | Deficit hit then‑record $264.9 bn                                  |
| **FY24–FY26 export plateau**         | Exports flat ~$437–442 bn; imports keep rising             | Deficit to successive records, $333 bn in FY26                     |

**Read:** historically the deficit shrank only when _imports_ fell (oil crash, COVID) — i.e. on the back of weakness, not export strength. The recent widening is different: it comes from exports _stalling_ while imports grow, which is a more structural concern than the price‑driven swings of the 2010s.

## 3. Export basket transformation

The composition of what India sells abroad has shifted decisively over 16 years — away from traditional resource‑ and labour‑intensive categories toward engineering, electronics, and chemicals.

**Share gainers (structural winners):**

| Group               | Share FY10 → FY26         | Value ($bn) |      CAGR | Note                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------: | --------: | ------------------------------------ |
| Electronics         | 3.1% → **10.9%** (+7.7pp) |  5.6 → 48.0 | **14.4%** | Fastest‑rising group; smartphone‑led |
| Machinery           | 5.6% → **11.1%** (+5.5pp) | 10.0 → 48.8 |     10.4% | **Now a top-tier export group**      |
| Chemicals & related | 9.6% → **14.0%** (+4.3pp) | 17.2 → 61.6 |      8.3% | Steady, broad‑based                  |
| Transport equipment | 5.7% → 7.6% (+1.9pp)      | 10.2 → 33.6 |      7.7% | Autos & components                   |
| Agri & allied       | 7.3% → 9.3% (+2.0pp)      | 13.0 → 41.0 |      7.5% |                                      |

**Share losers:**

| Group                 | Share FY10 → FY26         | Value ($bn) |      CAGR | Note                                                            |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------: | --------: | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gems & Jewellery**  | 16.1% → **6.4%** (−9.7pp) | 28.7 → 28.2 | **−0.1%** | Biggest loser; **absolute value essentially flat for 16 years** |
| **Textiles & allied** | 13.0% → **8.1%** (−4.9pp) | 23.3 → 35.7 |      2.7% | Grew slowly, lost relative ground                               |
| Petroleum products    | 15.8% → 12.2% (−3.6pp)    | 28.2 → 53.8 |      4.1% | Highly cyclical — peaked at **21.6% in FY23**                   |
| Ores & minerals       | 4.0% → 1.3% (−2.8pp)      |   7.2 → 5.5 |     −1.7% | Absolute decline                                                |

**The FY26 top of the basket** is now remarkably balanced across four pillars: **Chemicals 14.0%, Petroleum 12.2%, Machinery 11.1%, Electronics 10.9%** — a very different shape from FY10, when Gems (16.1%), Petroleum (15.8%) and Textiles (13.0%) dominated. India's export engine has moved up the value chain.

| Machinery                      | 8.4% → 9.2% (+0.8pp)       |  24.2 → 71.4 |      7.0% |                                          |

**Read:** oil still anchors the import bill but its _dominance_ is fading — its share dropped nearly 8 points. The slack was taken up almost one‑for‑one by **electronics**, which more than 5×'d to $116 bn. This is the import mirror of the export story: India's electronics boom is **assembly‑led and import‑dependent.**

## 6. Concentration

Imports are **more concentrated** than exports:

|         | Top‑5 share | Top‑10 share |
| ------- | ----------: | -----------: |
| Exports |       30.4% |        39.2% |
| Imports |   **37.4%** |    **47.3%** |

Nearly half of imports sit in ten HS‑4 lines dominated by bulk commodities (crude, gold, chips, coal, gas). Exports are spread across a broader, more diversified set of products — a relative strength, but also why no single export line can quickly close the deficit.

## 7. Currency overlay

|                        | FY11 | FY16 | FY21 |     FY26 |
| ---------------------- | ---: | ---: | ---: | -------: |
| INR per USD (FY avg)   | 45.6 | 65.5 | 74.2 | **88.3** |
| INR per USD (year‑end) | 44.6 | 66.3 | 73.5 | **94.7** |

- The rupee **depreciated ~1.9× (≈4.5% CAGR)** against the dollar over the period and continues to weaken (FY26 year‑end already ₹94.7).
- **Implication for the numbers above:** all trade values are in USD, so dollar‑terms export growth (5.8% CAGR) _understates_ rupee‑terms growth — in INR the export book grew roughly 4.8×. A weaker rupee cushions exporters but **inflates the USD‑priced import bill** (crude, gold, chips), reinforcing the deficit.
- A persistent merchandise deficit is one structural pressure on the currency, but the relationship is not one-to-one; inflation, capital flows, oil prices and global dollar cycles also matter.

## 8. Structural synthesis — what the long run says

1. **The deficit is the master trend.** It is permanent, widening, and increasingly _structural_ rather than oil‑price‑cyclical. Export coverage at a record‑low 57% (FY26) is the cleanest single warning indicator.
2. **Twin engines of the import bill have rotated.** Oil's share is shrinking; **electronics + precious metals** are filling the gap. The bill is becoming less about energy prices and more about structural demand for chips, gold, and assembled‑goods inputs.
3. **Electronics is the pivot category** — simultaneously India's **fastest‑rising export** (smartphones, +375%) _and_ a **fast‑rising import** (ICs +122%, components). The value chain is assembly‑led and import‑heavy, so export gains here partly self‑fund import growth. Rising domestic value‑addition is the variable to watch.
4. **The export basket has genuinely upgraded** — from gems/textiles/ores toward machinery/electronics/chemicals/pharma. This is durable diversification, even if it hasn't yet been fast enough to outpace imports.
5. **The FY24–FY26 export plateau is the key risk.** Three flat years (~$437–442 bn) while imports keep climbing is what tipped the deficit to records. Whether exports break above ~$450 bn is the single most important thing to watch next.

## Data notes & caveats

- **Merchandise only.** This is goods trade. India runs a large **services surplus** (software/IT, business services) that offsets a substantial part of this merchandise deficit — the _overall_ external balance is materially better than the goods picture here. Any deficit conclusion above is a goods‑trade statement, not a balance‑of‑payments one.
- **Full‑year totals & the summary CSV.** `data/india_trade_yearly_summary.csv` stores FTSPCC **full fiscal‑year totals**, taken from the March (fiscal year‑end) cumulative row for each year. (An earlier build step selected the highest _calendar‑month number_ — December = 12 — instead of the fiscal year‑end March = 3, undercounting every year by ~25–30%; this was fixed in `scripts/build_ftspcc_yearly.js` and the sibling fetch pipeline `scripts/fetch_ftspcc_yearly.js`.) For this note, the **headline 17‑year annual series comes from the FTPA commodity‑group totals**, which include FY 2009‑10. The FTSPCC summary CSV is a useful cross‑check for FY 2010‑11 onward, but it does not cover FY 2009‑10 and differs from the commodity/HS‑4 totals in some later years, notably FY 2023‑24 and provisional FY 2025‑26.
- **Reconciliation detail.** The HS‑4 totals used for the FY22–FY26 product analysis match the commodity‑group totals extremely closely through FY 2024‑25 and remain directionally consistent in provisional FY 2025‑26, but this is not a perfect three‑way identity across every series and year.
- **FY25‑26 is provisional** (latest reported year).
