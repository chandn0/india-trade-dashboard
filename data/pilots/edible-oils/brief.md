# Edible Oils Evidence Pilot Brief

**Pilot period:** FY 2025-26
**Products:** HS 1511 (Palm oil), HS 1507 (Soybean oil), HS 1512 (Sunflower oil), HS 1201 (Soya beans), HS 1205 (Rape/mustard seeds), HS 1202 (Groundnuts)
**Data sources:** EIDB DGFT, Ministry of Agriculture (NMEO-OP), CBIC; see sources.md

---

## Q1: Did the deficit change because of prices or physical dependence?

India imports roughly 15-16 million metric tonnes of edible oils annually. While the USD import bill fluctuates wildly, the physical volume remains structurally flat-to-growing.

**FY26 vs FY25 Dynamics:**
- **HS 1511 (Palm Oil):** Import bill dropped by 12.4%, but this was driven entirely by a **14.5% drop in global prices**. Physical import volume actually **increased by 2.5%** to 9.1 million MT.
- **HS 1507 (Soybean Oil):** Value increased by 4.2%, but physical volume spiked by 8.5%.
- **HS 1512 (Sunflower Oil):** Value increased by 15.6%, driven by a massive 22% surge in physical volumes.

**Conclusion:** The import deficit in edible oils is a structural physical dependency. When the import bill drops, it is almost always due to global commodity price deflation, not successful domestic substitution.

---

## Q2: What share could plausibly be grown domestically?

Currently, domestic production covers **43.74%** of total edible oil demand, leaving a **56.25%** import dependence. The import reliance for key oils is staggering:
- **Palm Oil (1511):** 96.8% imported
- **Sunflower Oil (1512):** 90.6% imported
- **Soybean Oil (1507):** 66.6% imported

Plausible domestic expansion is limited to the NMEO-OP, which targets 1M hectares (mostly in the North East and Andamans). Even if fully successful, this targets a total production of 2.8 million MT of crude palm oil by 2029-30 (an increase of ~2.42 million MT from the 2024-25 baseline), barely keeping pace with consumption growth.

---

## Q3: What is constrained by agroclimate, land, or food-price effects?

**Land & Yield Constraints:**
- **Soya beans (1201):** Yields remain stagnant at ~1 MT/ha (vs 3 MT/ha globally). Expansion competes directly with other Kharif crops. Lack of GM seed approval structurally limits yield leaps.
- **Mustard (1205):** Competes directly with wheat for acreage. Land area is near saturation.
- **Palm Oil (1511):** Highly water-intensive. Expansion outside the North East and Andamans is severely limited by agroclimatic realities and ecological concerns.

**Food-Price Effects:**
To protect domestic farmers, the government raised BCD to 27.5% on crude oils in late 2024 (subsequently reduced to 10% in June 2025). This trade-off balances farmer realisation against domestic food inflation, but does not solve the fundamental land-yield deficit.

---

## Q4: Is sourcing diversification more realistic than substitution?

Yes. Given the hard land constraints, absolute substitution is impossible. The strategic focus must shift to mitigating sourcing risks:
- **Palm Oil:** Indonesia 54%, Malaysia 30%.
- **Soybean Oil:** Argentina 54%, Brazil 40%.
- **Sunflower Oil:** Russia 45%, Ukraine 30%.

**Strategic Conclusion:** Edible oils should not be viewed as a solvable "import substitution" opportunity. The deficit is a hard geographic and ecological constraint.
