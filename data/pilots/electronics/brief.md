# Electronics Evidence Pilot Brief

**Pilot period:** FY 2025-26
**Products:** HS 8542 (ICs), HS 8541 (semiconductor devices), HS 8517 (telecom/phones), HS 8471 (computers), HS 8507 (batteries), HS 8473 (ADP parts), HS 8536 (switching apparatus)
**Data sources:** EIDB DGFT, MEITY PLI progress reports, India Semiconductor Mission; see sources.md

---

## Q1: Which component gaps widen as finished-electronics exports grow?

HS 8517 (smartphones) exports grew 4.8x in four years (FY22: $7.1bn -> FY26: $33.8bn), supported by PLI and broader industry investment. As a result, these component deficits worsened:

- HS 8542 Integrated circuits: net -$30.0 bn (gap widened +$16.7 bn in four years)
- HS 8507 Batteries/Li-ion: net -$4.9 bn (gap widened +$3.3 bn in four years)
- HS 8473 ADP parts: net -$3.5 bn (widening)

**Conclusion:** IC and battery import deficits are widening faster than any near-term domestic manufacturing can close. Smartphone export growth is structurally coupled to IC imports.

---

## Q2: Which imports are locally manufacturable vs structurally technology-dependent?

**Locally manufacturable:**
- HS 8517 Smartphones: ~99% of domestic demand assembled locally; PLI LSEM operational
- HS 8541 Solar PV (8541.42): ~25 GW/year domestic capacity under ALMM; Waaree, Adani, Premier Energies
- HS 8507 Li-ion cell assembly: PLI ACC 40 GWh awarded; only 1 GWh currently installed in pilot production (3-5 years to scale)
- HS 8536 Switching apparatus: Havells, L&T, Legrand as active domestic producers

**Structurally technology-dependent (not substitutable in <=10 years):**
- HS 8542 Logic and memory ICs: Wafer fabrication requires EUV lithography, $10-15bn fab capex, 10+ year learning curve. Micron ATMP (Gujarat) is packaging only, not logic wafer fab. Horizon: 10+ years for volume logic; memory possible 7-10 years.
- HS 8471 Laptops/servers: PCBs, SoCs, displays all imported; local value-add is assembly only.

**Interpretation change:** HS 8542 gross import rank (#1, $30.3bn) overstates near-term localisation potential. The correct frame is technology-dependent with a 7-10 year realistic horizon.

---

## Q3: Does the net electronics position improve under plausible localisation?

For the full electronics cluster (HS 8517 + 8542 + 8507), net position is approximately -$22 bn. Localising assembly (HS 8517) without concurrent IC and battery manufacturing generates export gains but also widens component deficits -- a structural treadmill.

Battery PLI (HS 8507) is the most near-term lever: 40 GWh by FY30 could reduce battery imports by $2-3 bn depending on EV demand trajectory.

---

## Q4: Which partner concentration is a sourcing risk vs a deficit issue?

- **Chapter 85 Proxy**: China (58%) + Hong Kong (13%) account for ~71% of the observed major-partner HS-85 import total.
  - **HS 8542**: While the chapter is highly concentrated, product-specific (HS-4) supplier concentration data is currently unavailable. Geopolitical disruption is a real supply risk for ICs, but the exact concentration cannot be definitively ranked without true HS-4 data. Alternatives (Korea Samsung/SK Hynix, USA Qualcomm/Broadcom) exist but cost more.
  - **HS 8517**: Despite chapter concentration, HS 8517 has a +$12bn surplus. **Not a deficit issue** -- risk is in components (8542, 8507), not finished phones.
  - **HS 8507**: **Concentration risk with emerging domestic alternative** -- PLI ACC is the correct policy response; Korea provides partial alternative.
- **Chapter 84 Proxy**: China accounts for ~56% of observed major-partner HS-84 imports.
  - **HS 8471**: Taiwan/Vietnam/Mexico provide realistic alternatives for branded laptops. **Deficit issue, not primarily concentration risk**.

## Success criterion

> The pilot changes the rank or interpretation of at least one electronics opportunity compared with gross-import ranking.

**Achieved for two products:**
1. HS 8517 moves from "large import" (would rank 2nd by gross import) to "net surplus, not a net-deficit reduction priority" -- correctly removed from import-substitution targets.
2. HS 8542 moves from "substitutable domestic production opportunity" to "technology-dependent, 10-year horizon, component-gap risk for export growth" -- fundamentally different characterisation.
